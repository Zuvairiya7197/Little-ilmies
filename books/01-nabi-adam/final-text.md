## Page 3 — Introduction to Parents
All praise is due to Allah, Lord of the worlds. May peace and blessings be upon the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ, his family, and all his companions.

Dear Parents,

This book has been written to help you teach your child about the story of Adam ʿalayhis-salām — the first human, whom Allah chose and guided, as described in the Qur'an and authentic Sunnah. Understanding the story of Adam is part of the correct Aqeedah and lays a foundation for loving Tawheed, following the guidance of Allah, and learning valuable lessons from the Prophets.

In today's time, many children may hear stories that are not based on authentic sources. This book presents the life and lessons of Adam ʿalayhis-salām in simple words, using only verses from the Qur'an and hadith from Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim, upon the understanding of the Salaf-us-Salih. The book itself speaks to your child as a storyteller, so it can be read aloud warmly, page by page.

## Page 4 — A Note for Parents: Why Some Details Are Not in This Book
Dear Parents,

You may have heard or read the story of Adam (ʿalayhis-salām) before — in other storybooks, cartoons, lectures, or from family. If so, you may notice that some well-known details are not in this book. We left them out on purpose.

In this book, we have only included what is confirmed in the Qur'an, Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim. We have not included details that come from the Isrā'īliyyāt (reports taken from the People of the Book), or from weak or fabricated narrations.

Some examples of what you may have heard, but will not find here:
1. The names "Habil" and "Qabil" (Abel and Cain). The Qur'an only calls them "the two sons of Adam" (5:27). These names come from the People of the Book. Some English translations add them in brackets; we have removed them.
2. Ḥawwā' giving birth to twins — a boy and a girl each time — and the brothers marrying each other's twin sisters. This comes from Isrā'īliyyāt.
3. The two sons' offerings — one offering his best sheep and the other his worst crops — and the claim that the quarrel was about whom to marry. The Qur'an only tells us that each offered a sacrifice, and it was accepted from one and not the other.
4. The tree being wheat, grapes, figs or another fruit. Allah did not tell us which tree it was, so we do not name it.
5. Iblīs sneaking into Paradise hidden in the mouth of a snake or a peacock. This is from Isrā'īliyyāt.
6. Adam being sent down to India (or Sri Lanka) and Ḥawwā' to Jeddah, and the two meeting again at ʿArafah. None of this is confirmed in the Qur'an, Sahih al-Bukhari or Sahih Muslim.
7. Adam crying for hundreds of years after leaving Paradise. This is not authentic.
8. Adam asking Allah for forgiveness "by the right of Muhammad ﷺ". Scholars of hadith have judged this narration to be fabricated. The words Adam was taught are those in the Qur'an: "Our Lord! We have wronged ourselves…" (7:23).
9. Shīth (Seth) being born to comfort Adam after the killing, and carrying on his teaching. This is not established by authentic texts.
10. The angels washing Adam's body and burying him, and the details of his grave. These narrations are disputed, so we left them out.

Why are we so careful?
The Prophet ﷺ said: "Do not believe the People of the Book, and do not disbelieve them, but say: 'We believe in Allah and what has been revealed to us…'" (Sahih al-Bukhari 4485)

Allah has given us enough in His Book and in the authentic Sunnah. Our children's first picture of the Prophets should be built on certainty, not on stories that may be untrue.

A small note: some narrations about Adam are authentic but are found in collections other than Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim. One example is Adam giving forty years of his life to Dāwūd, narrated by at-Tirmidhī (3076), who graded it Hasan Sahih. To keep this book simple and as certain as possible, we chose to use only the Qur'an, Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim.

May Allah keep our children upon the truth, and make them love His Prophets.

## Page 6 — The Creation of Adam (ʿalayhis-salām)
Assalāmu ʿalaykum, little friend! Yes, it's me — your book! Sit somewhere cosy, because I have the most amazing true story to tell you. Everything I tell you comes from the Qur'an and the authentic Sunnah, so you can trust me: this really happened.

Long, long ago, Allah created the heavens and the earth. He also created the angels from light. The angels always obey Allah.

> They fear their Lord above them, and they do what they are commanded. — (Surah An-Naḥl 16:50)

> The Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: "The angels were created from light, the jinn were created from smokeless fire, and Adam was created from what has been described to you." — (Sahih Muslim 2996)

Then Allah told the angels something very important:

> And (remember) when your Lord said to the angels: "Verily, I am going to place (mankind) generations after generations on earth." They said: "Will You place therein those who will make mischief therein and shed blood, - while we glorify You with praises and thanks and sanctify You." He (Allâh) said: "I know that which you do not know." — (Surah Al-Baqarah 2:30)

The angels asked a question. They were not arguing; they only wanted to understand. And Allah answered: "I know that which you do not know." Allah knows everything, and His plan is always perfect.

## Page 7 — Made from the Earth
Can you guess what Allah used to create the very first human? Not gold. Not jewels. Earth!

People today come in so many different colours and speak so many different languages. Every one of them is a sign of Allah!

> And among His Signs is the creation of the heavens and the earth, and the difference of your languages and colours. Verily, in that are indeed signs for men of sound knowledge. — (Surah Ar-Rūm 30:22)

Let's count together! Here are the ways Allah describes, in the Qur'an, the earth that Adam was made from:

1. Dust (turāb) — "…He created him from dust, then (He) said to him: "Be!" - and he was." (Surah Āl ʿImrān 3:59)
2. Clay (ṭīn) — "(Remember) when your Lord said to the angels: "Truly I am going to create man from clay"." (Surah Ṣād 38:71)
3. Sticky clay (ṭīn lāzib) — "…Verily, We created them of a sticky clay." (Surah Aṣ-Ṣāffāt 37:11)
4. Altered mud (ḥama' masnūn) — "And indeed, We created man from dried (sounding) clay of altered mud." (Surah Al-Ḥijr 15:26)
5. Dry clay that rings when you tap it, like a clay pot (ṣalṣāl) — "He created man (Adam) from sounding clay like the clay of pottery." (Surah Ar-Raḥmān 55:14)

## Page 8 — Adam Comes to Life
Then Allah shaped Adam with His own Hands. What an honour! Then Allah breathed into him the soul that He created for him, and Adam came to life. He was the very first human being, the father of all people. That means he is your great-great-great… grandfather too!

> "So, when I have fashioned him completely and breathed into him (Adam) the soul which I created for him, then fall (you) down prostrating yourselves unto him." — (Surah Al-Ḥijr 15:29)

> (Allâh) said: "O Iblîs (Satan)! What prevents you from prostrating yourself to one whom I have created with Both My Hands. Are you too proud (to fall prostrate to Adam) or are you one of the high exalted?" — (Surah Ṣād 38:75)

Adam was very tall: sixty cubits! That's much, much taller than any person today.

Then Allah told Adam to greet a group of angels. Adam said, "As-salāmu ʿalaykum!" The angels answered, "As-salāmu ʿalayka wa raḥmatullāh." So every time you say "As-salāmu ʿalaykum", you are using the greeting of our father Adam!

> The Prophet ﷺ said, "Allah created Adam, making him 60 cubits tall. When He created him, He said to him, "Go and greet that group of angels, and listen to their reply, for it will be your greeting (salutation) and the greeting (salutation) of your offspring." So, Adam said (to the angels), As-Salamu Alaikum (i.e. Peace be upon you). The angels said, "As-salamu Alaika wa Rahmatu-l-lahi" (i.e. Peace and Allah's Mercy be upon you). Thus the angels added to Adam's salutation the expression, 'Wa Rahmatu-l-lahi.' Any person who will enter Paradise will resemble Adam (in appearance and figure). People have been decreasing in stature since Adam's creation." — (Sahih al-Bukhari 3326)

## Page 9 — The Big Promise
Now I have something amazing to tell you. Allah brought out all the children of Adam — every person who would ever be born — and that includes you! Allah asked them: "Am I not your Lord?" And they all said: "Yes! We testify!"

> And (remember) when your Lord brought forth from the Children of Adam, from their loins, their seed (or from Adam's loin his offspring) and made them testify as to themselves (saying): "Am I not your Lord?" They said: "Yes! We testify," lest you should say on the Day of Resurrection: "Verily, we have been unaware of this." — (Surah Al-Aʿrāf 7:172)

That's why, deep inside, every child is born knowing that Allah is their Lord.

> The Prophet ﷺ said: "Every child is born upon the fiṭrah (the natural way of Islam)…" — (Sahih al-Bukhari 1385)

## Page 10 — The Gift of Knowledge
Do you like learning new words? Then you'll love this part!

Allah taught Adam the names of everything. Then Allah showed those things to the angels and said, "Tell Me their names." The angels humbly said, "We only know what You have taught us." Then Adam told them all the names!

Knowledge is a precious gift from Allah, and Allah honoured Adam with it.

> And He taught Adam all the names (of everything), then He showed them to the angels and said, "Tell Me the names of these if you are truthful." — (Surah Al-Baqarah 2:31)

> They (angels) said: "Glory be to You, we have no knowledge except what you have taught us. Verily, it is You, the All-Knower, the All-Wise." — (Surah Al-Baqarah 2:32)

> He said: "O Adam! Inform them of their names," and when he had informed them of their names, He said: "Did I not tell you that I know the Ghaib (Unseen) in the heavens and the earth, and I know what you reveal and what you have been concealing?" — (Surah Al-Baqarah 2:33)

## Page 11 — The Command
Then Allah commanded the angels: "Prostrate to Adam." All the angels obeyed — except one: Iblīs.

> And (remember) when We said to the angels: "Prostrate yourselves before Adam." And they prostrated except Iblîs (Satan), he refused and was proud and was one of the disbelievers (disobedient to Allâh). — (Surah Al-Baqarah 2:34)

> So the angels prostrated themselves, all of them together. — (Surah Al-Ḥijr 15:30)

Iblīs was not an angel. He was from the jinn, and the jinn are created from fire.

> And (remember) when We said to the angels: "Prostrate yourselves unto Adam." So they prostrated themselves except Iblîs (Satan). He was one of the jinn; he disobeyed the Command of his Lord… — (Surah Al-Kahf 18:50)

Little note: The angels prostrated because Allah commanded them to. In our religion, we prostrate only to Allah.

> And from among His Signs are the night and the day, and the sun and the moon. Prostrate yourselves not to the sun nor to the moon, but prostrate yourselves to Allâh Who created them, if you (really) worship Him. — (Surah Fuṣṣilat 41:37)

## Page 12 — The Proud One
Allah asked Iblīs why he did not prostrate. Do you know what Iblīs said? "I am better than him!" That is pride — thinking you are better than others. Pride made Iblīs disobey Allah.

> (Allâh) said: "What prevented you (O Iblîs) that you did not prostrate yourself, when I commanded you?" Iblîs said: "I am better than him (Adam), You created me from fire, and him You created from clay." — (7:12)
> (Allâh) said: "(O Iblîs) get down from this (Paradise), it is not for you to be arrogant here. Get out, for you are of those humiliated and disgraced." — (7:13)
> (Iblîs) said: "Allow me respite till the Day they are raised up (i.e. the Day of Resurrection)." — (7:14)
> (Allâh) said: "You are of those respited." — (7:15)
> (Iblîs) said: "Because You have sent me astray, surely I will sit in wait against them (human beings) on Your Straight Path. — (7:16)
> "Then I will come to them from before them and behind them, from their right and from their left, and You will not find most of them as thankful ones (i.e. they will not be dutiful to You)." — (7:17)
> (Allâh) said (to Iblîs): "Get out from this (Paradise), disgraced and expelled. Whoever of them (mankind) will follow you, then surely I will fill Hell with you all." — (7:18)
(Surah Al-Aʿrāf 7:12–18)

So Allah sent Iblīs out, disgraced. Iblīs became the enemy of Adam and of all his children. He promised to try to trick people from the front, from behind, from the right and from the left.

But here's the good news: Allah promised that whoever follows His guidance will have no fear and no sadness. (Surah Al-Baqarah 2:38)

## Page 13 — A Home in Paradise
Allah created a wife for Adam from Adam himself, and her name was Ḥawwā'.

> O mankind! Be dutiful to your Lord, Who created you from a single person (Adam), and from him (Adam) He created his wife [Hawwâ (Eve)], and from them both He created many men and women… — (Surah An-Nisā' 4:1)

Adam and Ḥawwā' lived in Jannah — a garden more beautiful than anything you could ever imagine. They could eat from anything they wished. There was just one rule: don't go near one tree.

> "And O Adam! Dwell you and your wife in Paradise, and eat thereof as you both wish, but approach not this tree otherwise you both will be of the Zâlimûn (unjust and wrong-doers)." — (Surah Al-Aʿrāf 7:19)

## Page 14 — The Sneaky Whisper
But Shayṭān didn't give up. He whispered tricky words: "Your Lord only stopped you from this tree so you wouldn't become angels or live forever." He even swore by Allah: "I'm only trying to help you!" Every word was a lie.

Adam and Ḥawwā' were tricked, and they ate from the tree. Straight away, their bodies became uncovered, and they quickly covered themselves with leaves from Paradise.

Then Allah called them: "Did I not forbid you that tree, and tell you that Shayṭān is your open enemy?"

> Then Shaitân (Satan) whispered suggestions to them both in order to uncover that which was hidden from them of their private parts (before); he said: "Your Lord did not forbid you this tree save that you should become angels or become of the immortals." — (7:20)
> And he [Shaitân (Satan)] swore by Allâh to them both (saying): "Verily, I am one of the sincere well-wishers for you both." — (7:21)
> So he misled them with deception. Then when they tasted of the tree, that which was hidden from them of their shame (private parts) became manifest to them and they began to cover themselves with the leaves of Paradise (in order to cover their shame). And their Lord called out to them (saying): "Did I not forbid you that tree and tell you: Verily, Shaitân (Satan) is an open enemy unto you?" — (7:22)
(Surah Al-Aʿrāf 7:20–22)

## Page 15 — Saying Sorry to Allah
Now, little friend, this is the most important part of the story — so listen carefully.

When Adam and Ḥawwā' made a mistake, they didn't make excuses. They turned to Allah straight away and said, "We have wronged ourselves."

> They said: "Our Lord! We have wronged ourselves. If You forgive us not, and bestow not upon us Your Mercy, we shall certainly be of the losers." — (Surah Al-Aʿrāf 7:23)

And Allah — at-Tawwāb (the One who accepts repentance), ar-Raḥīm (the Most Merciful) — forgave them!

Then Allah sent them down to the earth. Earth would be their home for a while. And Allah promised that whoever follows His guidance will have no fear and no sadness.

> Then the Shaitân (Satan) made them slip therefrom (the Paradise), and got them out from that in which they were. We said: "Get you down, all, with enmity between yourselves. On earth will be a dwelling place for you and an enjoyment for a time." — (2:36)
> Then Adam received from his Lord Words. And his Lord pardoned him (accepted his repentance). Verily, He is the One Who forgives (accepts repentance), the Most Merciful. — (2:37)
> We said: "Get down all of you from this place (the Paradise), then whenever there comes to you Guidance from Me, and whoever follows My Guidance, there shall be no fear on them, nor shall they grieve." — (2:38)
> But those who disbelieve and belie Our Ayât (proofs, evidence, verses, lessons, signs, revelations, etc.) - such are the dwellers of the Fire. They shall abide therein forever. — (2:39)
(Surah Al-Baqarah 2:36–39)

## Page 16 — Chosen by Allah
After Adam said sorry, Allah chose him and guided him.

> Then his Lord chose him, and turned to him with forgiveness, and gave him guidance. — (Surah Ṭā Hā 20:122)

Adam and Ḥawwā' lived on the earth, and from them Allah spread many men and women.

Allah tells us about two of Adam's sons. Each of them offered something to Allah. Allah accepted it from one son, but not from the other. The son whose offering wasn't accepted said, "I will surely kill you!" But his brother answered with beautiful words: "Allah accepts only from those who fear Him."

> And (O Muhammad ﷺ) recite to them (the Jews) the story of the two sons of Adam in truth; when each offered a sacrifice (to Allâh), it was accepted from the one but not from the other. The latter said to the former: "I will surely kill you." The former said: "Verily, Allâh accepts only from those who are Al-Muttaqûn (the pious)." — (5:27)
> "If you do stretch your hand against me to kill me, I shall never stretch my hand against you to kill you : for I fear Allâh, the Lord of the ‘Âlamîn (mankind, jinn, and all that exists)." — (5:28)
> "Verily, I intend to let you draw my sin on yourself as well as yours, then you will be one of the dwellers of the Fire; and that is the recompense of the Zâlimûn (polytheists and wrong-doers)." — (5:29)
(Surah Al-Mā'idah 5:27–29)

## Page 17 — The First Wrong on Earth
The good brother would not fight back, because he feared Allah. But the other brother's own self (nafs) made the evil deed seem good to him, and he killed his own brother. It was the first killing on earth.

Then he didn't know what to do. Allah sent a crow, which scratched the ground to show him how to bury his brother. He said, "Woe to me! I couldn't even be like this crow!" And he became full of regret.

> So the Nafs (self) of the other (latter one) encouraged him and made fair-seeming to him the murder of his brother; he murdered him and became one of the losers. — (5:30)
> Then Allâh sent a crow who scratched the ground to show him to hide the dead body of his brother. He (the murderer) said: "Woe to me! Am I not even able to be as this crow and to hide the dead body of my brother?" Then he became one of those who regretted. — (5:31)
(Surah Al-Mā'idah 5:30–31)

> The Prophet ﷺ said: "No person is killed unjustly except that the first son of Adam bears a share of its burden, because he was the first to start the practice of killing." — (Sahih al-Bukhari 3335)

You see, little friend: we must never let our nafs push us to do wrong. Instead, let's love good for others, just as we love it for ourselves.

> The Prophet ﷺ said: "None of you truly believes until he loves for his brother what he loves for himself." — (Sahih al-Bukhari 13)

## Page 18 — The Father of Us All
Adam lived on the earth until the time Allah had written for him — because every soul will taste death.

> Everyone shall taste death… — (Surah Āl ʿImrān 3:185)

Do you know which day is the best day of the week? Friday! On a Friday Adam was created, on a Friday he entered Jannah, and on a Friday he was taken out of it.

> The Prophet ﷺ said: "The best day on which the sun has risen is Friday. On it Adam was created, on it he was made to enter Paradise, and on it he was taken out of it. And the Hour will not be established except on a Friday." — (Sahih Muslim 854)

On the Day of Judgement, people will come to Adam and remember how Allah honoured him:

> "…They will go to Adam and say: 'You are the father of mankind. Allah created you with His Own Hands, ordered the angels to prostrate to you, and taught you the names of all things…'" — (Sahih al-Bukhari 4476)

And that's the end of my story, little friend. Now, shall we look at what we learned?

## Pages 19–20 — What I Learned from Nabi Adam (ʿalayhis-salām)
1. Allah is the Creator: Allah made Adam from the earth. Our different colours are signs of Allah.
2. We were born knowing Allah: Every child of Adam said, "Yes, You are our Lord!"
3. Knowledge is a gift: Allah taught Adam the names of everything. Let's love learning!
4. Obey Allah straight away: The angels obeyed at once.
5. Pride is dangerous: Iblīs said "I am better," and lost everything. Be humble.
6. Spread salām: Our greeting comes from Adam. Spread salām among Muslims — to those you know and those you don't know. (Sahih al-Bukhari 12)
7. Allah's rules are for our good: There was just one rule, and following it was best.
8. Shayṭān is our enemy: He lies and tricks. Don't listen to his whispers.
9. Everyone makes mistakes: Even our father Adam slipped.
10. Say sorry to Allah quickly: Adam and Ḥawwā' said, "We have wronged ourselves," and Allah forgave them.
11. Allah is the Most Merciful: Allah loves to forgive those who turn back to Him.
12. Be like the good brother: He feared Allah and would not do wrong.
13. Don't follow your nafs when it tells you to do wrong: Love for others what you love for yourself.
14. Friday is the best day: Send lots of ṣalawāt on the Prophet ﷺ. Whoever sends ṣalawāt once, Allah blesses them ten times. (Sahih Muslim 408)

## Page 22 — Duʿā' to Memorise
Duʿā' for Forgiveness (to say in your prayer)

اللّهُـمَّ إِنِّـي ظَلَـمْتُ نَفْسـي ظُلْمـاً كَثـيراً وَلا يَغْـفِرُ الذُّنـوبَ إِلاّ أَنْت، فَاغْـفِر لي مَغْـفِرَةً مِنْ عِنْـدِك وَارْحَمْـني، إِنَّكَ أَنْتَ الغَـفورُ الرَّحـيم

Allāhumma 'innī ẓalamtu nafsī ẓulman kathīran, wa lā yaghfiru-dhdhunūba illā 'anta, faghfir lī maghfiratam min `indika warḥamnī innaka 'anta 'l-Ghafūr ur-Raḥīm.

Translation: O Allah, I have greatly wronged myself, and no one forgives sins but You. So, grant me forgiveness and have mercy on me. Surely, You are the Forgiving, the Merciful.

Reference: Sahih al-Bukhari 834, Sahih Muslim 2705
